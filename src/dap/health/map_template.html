Preventable hospital admissions: higher or lower than expected?

Each area is an SA3 (an ABS region of roughly 30,000 to 130,000 people). The default view compares each area's potentially preventable hospitalisation rate in $year with the rate a model expected from the area's social, demographic and access profile. Hover over or tap an area for its numbers.

    How to read this

    • Expected comes from a $model model trained on $features input features (disadvantage, income support, housing, age, birthplace, remoteness, distance to hospital, GP use and screening). Each area's expected rate was predicted while the area's whole SA4 region was held out of training, so the model never saw that area or its closest neighbours.
    • $within_10 of people live in an SA3 within 10% of its expected rate. $above_20 SA3s are more than 20% above expected and $below_20 are more than 20% below.
    • Remote areas: tree models can't predict above the highest rates they were trained on, so some very remote areas look further above expected than they really are.
    • Grey areas have no published rate. The AIHW suppresses rates for areas with very small numbers, and a few of those (Barkly, East Arnhem, West Pilbara) are remote areas that probably have some of the highest rates in the country.
    • Hot and cold spots are local Moran's I clusters at p < 0.05, not adjusted for testing hundreds of areas at once, so they show where to look rather than confirmed clusters.
    • This is area-level data. It describes places, not people, and "higher than expected" doesn't mean anyone there got worse care.

    The full method, results and limitations are in the project write-up.

    Data: AIHW potentially preventable hospitalisations by SA3 ($year), AIHW Medicare GP data and MyHospitals, PHIDU Social Health Atlas of Australia (Torrens University Australia), and ABS boundaries, remoteness areas and SEIFA.
